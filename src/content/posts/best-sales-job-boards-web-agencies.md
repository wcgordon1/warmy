## Key Takeaways

- **Choose the job before the board.** A new-business closer, client account manager, and appointment setter need different briefs. State which work this person owns.
- **Use Account Executive Jobs for a defined sales vacancy.** Its sales focus and reusable posting slots make it worth evaluating for an agency AE. That does not establish a higher placement rate.
- **Keep the application path short.** Give candidates the offer, responsibilities, pay structure, and next step. A vague agency pitch gives them little to evaluate.
- **Use MCP to prepare and manage the process.** Account Executive Jobs documents an AI connection for job search and employer workflows. Confirm changes before they happen.
- **Measure relevant conversations.** Track applicants against a written role scorecard. A large number of applications is not evidence that a channel fits the role.

## What an agency account executive actually does

An agency account executive owns defined commercial work. That can include qualifying opportunities, diagnosing the client’s problem, building a proposal with delivery colleagues, and closing a suitable project. The job needs a clear handoff into production.

An account manager often owns the relationship after the sale. A sales development representative usually starts conversations and qualifies interest. A partnerships role develops referral relationships. Small agencies sometimes combine these duties, but the job post still needs to describe the combination honestly.

Start with the offer the person will sell. A fixed website package, custom software engagement, and recurring maintenance plan have different buying conversations. Give candidates enough context to understand the service, typical stakeholders, and sales process.

Do not make the AE responsible for fixing an offer that has never sold. First document what the founder has learned from real opportunities. Write down why suitable clients buy, where projects stall, and what delivery can support. Those notes are the basis for a role brief and an interview scorecard.

## The Best Sales Job Boards for Web Agencies in 2026

### 1. Account Executive Jobs, best for a defined agency AE vacancy

[Account Executive Jobs](https://accountexecutivejobs.com/) focuses on account executive and sales roles. An agency can explain its service, territory, compensation structure, and qualification requirements without positioning the vacancy as a generic office job.

The [pricing page](https://accountexecutivejobs.com/pricing) currently lists Solo at $249 per month for one active job slot, Team at $599 for three, and Growth at $899 for five. A listing runs for 30 days. The subscription renews monthly until canceled, and listing expiration does not cancel billing. Choose capacity based on simultaneous vacancies rather than the number of people on your team.

The documented employer workflow includes private drafts, screening questions, application review, and notes. Use those tools around an agency-specific scorecard. For example, ask how a candidate would qualify a website request with an unclear budget and a fixed launch deadline. Evaluate the response against requirements you wrote before reviewing applicants.

**Best suited for:** Agencies that know what the AE will sell and want a focused sales hiring destination. It is less useful when the real vacancy is an operations coordinator or a client support role.

### 2. LinkedIn, best for a search built around professional context

[LinkedIn’s official hiring page](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/post-jobs) describes job posting, promotion, and broader recruiting products. It is worth evaluating when previous agency relationships, industry experience, or a candidate’s professional context matters to the search.

An agency should choose the product and budget for its actual search. Do not assume that a standard job post includes every recruiting feature advertised elsewhere in LinkedIn’s hiring product family. Review the current posting and promotion terms before opening a campaign.

Use the same role brief you publish elsewhere. If one version says “new business” and another emphasizes existing client service, applicants will enter different conversations. Keep the offer, pay structure, location, and ownership of the sales cycle consistent.

**Best suited for:** Agencies hiring across sales, partnerships, and account management, or using an established professional network. The hiring team must still evaluate job-related evidence consistently.

### 3. Indeed, best for testing a broader local hiring channel

[Indeed for Employers](https://www.indeed.com/hire) documents free posting subject to conditions and usage limits, plus paid Sponsored Jobs. It can be a useful additional channel when a local agency wants to test a broader search.

Write the job title in language candidates recognize. Explain whether the person sells projects, manages accounts, or books meetings. Describe the location and work arrangement clearly. Do not use an “account executive” title for a role whose daily work is unrelated to sales.

Review the available budget options in the posting flow. Do not treat a variable sponsored campaign as a fixed monthly product directly comparable to a reusable slot. Record spend and qualified applications separately so the agency can evaluate its own results.

**Best suited for:** Agencies with a clear local role and enough time to review a broader application pool. It can complement a specialist board without replacing a clear hiring brief.

## What a useful agency hiring brief looks like

An illustrative brief might say: “Own discovery through signed proposal for our website service. Work with delivery on scope. Hand over the agreed pages, assumptions, and client responsibilities before kickoff.” This is an example, not a customer result.

Build the scorecard around that work. Ask every candidate to explain a comparable sales situation and the handoff they created. Record evidence against the same criteria. After publication, count applications, suitable conversations, and progress through your own process without promising a result in advance.

Agencies that advise other employers can also review the [Account Executive Jobs affiliate program](https://accountexecutivejobs.com/affiliate). Its public terms currently describe 30% recurring commission on eligible active paid subscriptions and a 90-day tracking window. Affiliate eligibility and hiring performance are separate questions.
